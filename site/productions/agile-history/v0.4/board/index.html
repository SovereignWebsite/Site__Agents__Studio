  2. 2 It did not begin in 2001 roots, 0:07 to 0:25, 18 s, lab scene currents

    It did not begin in 2001. Larman and Basili trace iterative development back to the nineteen thirties, to short improvement cycles at Bell Labs. One programmer later recalled doing incremental development as early as 1957. Then, in 1970, came Winston Royce's paper.

    On screen: 1930s: Short improvement cycles; 1957: Incremental software, as later recalled; 1970: Royce's paper

  3. 3 Blamed for the waterfall royce, 0:25 to 0:37, 11 s, lab scene royce-cascade

    Blamed for the waterfall. Yet of that simple sequence of phases, Royce himself wrote: I believe in this concept, but the implementation described above is risky and invites failure.

    On screen: “I believe in this concept, but the implementation described above is risky and invites failure.” (Winston Royce, 1970)

  4. 4 The 1990s: lightweight methods methods, 0:37 to 0:55, 18 s, lab scene currents

    The nineteen nineties brought lightweight methods. DSDM, in 1994. Scrum, first presented in 1995. Extreme Programming, from 1996. Crystal, 1998. Feature-Driven Development, 1999. Lightweight was a label nobody really liked.

    On screen: 1994: DSDM; 1995: Scrum; 1996: Extreme Programming; 1998: Crystal; 1999: Feature-Driven Development

    In February 2001, seventeen people met at Snowbird, a ski resort in Utah, looking for common ground.

    On screen: Kent Beck, Mike Beedle, Arie van Bennekum, Alistair Cockburn, Ward Cunningham, Martin Fowler, James Grenning, Jim Highsmith, Andrew Hunt, Ron Jeffries, Jon Kern, Brian Marick, Robert C. Martin, Steve Mellor, Ken Schwaber, Jeff Sutherland, Dave Thomas

  6. 6 They chose a word: agile the-word, 1:02 to 1:12, 10 s, lab scene snowbird-page

    On screen: “a bigger gathering of organizational anarchists would be hard to find” (Jim Highsmith, 2001)

    On screen: Individuals and interactions over processes and tools; Working software over comprehensive documentation; Customer collaboration over contract negotiation; Responding to change over following a plan

  8. 8 Then it spread spread, 1:27 to 1:49, 22 s, lab scene spread

    Then it spread. The Agile Alliance formed in late 2001. ScrumMaster courses began in 2002. The first devopsdays was held in 2009. SAFe dates from 2011. The UK government's service manual says digital services must be agile. And since 2020, a United States defence instruction requires iterative methods on its software pathway.

    On screen: 2001: The Agile Alliance; 2002: ScrumMaster courses; 2009: DevOps; 2011: SAFe; 2016: UK government; 2020: US Defense

  9. 9 An industry industry, 1:49 to 2:04, 15 s, lab scene badge-factory

    On screen: 1.8 million: Scrum Alliance; 1.2 million: Scrum.org; 1 million: Scaled Agile. Members, certifications and people trained: their own figures, not audited.
