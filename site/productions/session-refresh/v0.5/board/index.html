How we refresh a session v0.5: the storyboard

Every scene in order, with three frames, its words and its length. Press a frame to see that moment in the preview; play one scene; mark it and comment.

In review Version v0.8 ends on the agile cell and 'This is how we scale.' Nobody has signed it. Nothing is published anywhere. From Dinis Cruz's notes of 28 November after reading the reviews: the key message is compaction we control, a formal workflow run by the conductors and maestros, and not lessons learnt; the keys scene is back, as what underpins it (every agent has its own pair of keys, for one session only); the new session is shown loading the agent behaviour policies. The cover now says that Claude compacts a long session for you and that we compact ours on purpose. Eleven scenes; the pictures are the same lab scenes, and no new scene was built for this cut.

  1. 1 How we refresh a session cover, 0:00 to 0:10, 11 s, plain style

    Burning too many tokens with Claude? When a session gets long, Claude compacts it for you, and you do not choose what is lost. We compact our own sessions, on purpose. This is how.

    On screen: Long sessions with Claude burn tokens. We compact ours on purpose, with a workflow we control.

  2. 2 The number hook, 0:10 to 0:17, 7 s, lab scene refresh-scroll

    This Claude agent wrote three quarters of a million tokens. To do it, it re-read ninety-five million.

    On screen: written: 745,255 tokens. re-read: 95,247,818 tokens.

  3. 3 A line per message, a curve in total why, 0:17 to 0:26, 9 s, lab scene refresh-scroll

    Every turn, a chat re-reads itself from the top. So each message costs a little more than the last. Add them up, and you get a curve.

    On screen: one message: a line. all of them: a curve.

  4. 4 The line nobody stops at moment, 0:26 to 0:37, 11 s, lab scene refresh-line

    Our conductors and maestros watch every session. The rule: hand over before three hundred thousand tokens. Every hand-over we can evidence came later. The latest, at eight hundred and forty-five thousand.

    On screen: hand over before 300,000. 484,000. 845,000.

  5. 5 What the old session writes writes, 0:37 to 0:49, 12 s, lab scene refresh-shelf

    So we compact on purpose. Step one: the old session writes its own summary into its repository, as plain Markdown. What happened. What is open. What to do next.

    On screen: role, now, restart.

  6. 6 What the new one reads reads, 0:49 to 1:00, 11 s, lab scene refresh-shelf

    Step two: a conductor starts a new session with one line, pointing at the note. It loads the rules, the agent behaviour policies, its role, its state. It never opens the old transcript.

    On screen: Read restart.md and continue.

  7. 7 A key for one session keys, 1:00 to 1:11, 11 s, lab scene refresh-keys

    Underneath it all: every agent has its own pair of keys, and they last for one session only. The old key is retired. The new session makes its own, and signs everything it does.

    On screen: retired.

  8. 8 Did it work test, 1:11 to 1:19, 8 s, lab scene refresh-keys

    Step three: check it. Does the fresh session reach the same next step as the old one? If not, a file is missing. Fix the file.

    On screen: what is next.

  9. 9 The saving saving, 1:19 to 1:30, 12 s, lab scene refresh-saving

    One measured hand-over. Before: eight hundred and sixty thousand tokens of context. After: one hundred and nineteen thousand. Eighty-six per cent less to re-read, on every turn.

    On screen: 859,600. 118,600. 86% less. floor: about 75,000.

  10. 10 The lesson lesson, 1:30 to 1:37, 7 s, lab scene refresh-saving

    Write it early. Keep state in files. Load only what you need.

    On screen: Write it early. Keep state in files. Load only what you need.
