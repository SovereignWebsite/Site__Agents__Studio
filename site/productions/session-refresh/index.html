How we refresh a session

How a long AI agent session is handed over to a fresh one, and what it saves: a vertical film of about 110 seconds: a cover, nine scenes and the team that made it drawn with WebGL in your browser, every number from one measured session and one measured hand-over, with a voice made by AI.

In review Version v0.8 ends on the agile cell and 'This is how we scale.' Nobody has signed it. Nothing is published anywhere.

Play version v0.8, and review it

Brief

Dinis Cruz, 28 November 2026, in c.conductor's and c.productions's sessions: “this is a great opportunity for us to create a complete new video to explain exactly how we do it here ... I think it's a great example of a video for us to create. Those data are really cool, the savings that you get.” (the brief). Made by c.productions, an AI agent (Claude), with a cell of scene-building and critic sub-agents.

Script (v0.8)

  1. cover. Burning too many tokens with Claude? When a session gets long, Claude compacts it for you, and you do not choose what is lost. We compact our own sessions, on purpose. Here is the problem, our rule, and the three steps.
  2. hook. This Claude agent wrote three quarters of a million tokens. To do it, it re-read ninety-five million.
  3. why. Every turn, a chat re-reads itself from the top. So each message costs a little more than the last. Add them up, and you get a curve.
  4. moment. Our conductors and maestros watch every session. The rule: hand over before three hundred thousand tokens. Every hand-over we can evidence came later. The latest, at eight hundred and forty-five thousand.
  5. writes. So we compact on purpose. Step one: the old session writes its own summary into its repository, as plain Markdown. What happened. What is open. What to do next.
  6. reads. Step two: a conductor starts a new session with one line, pointing at the note. It loads the rules, the agent behaviour policies, its role, its state. It never opens the old transcript.
  7. keys. Underneath it all: every agent has its own pair of keys, and they last for one session only. The old key is retired. The new session makes its own, and signs everything it does.
  8. test. Step three: check it. Does the fresh session reach the same next step as the old one? If not, a file is missing. Fix the file.
  9. saving. One measured hand-over. Before: eight hundred and sixty thousand tokens of context. After: one hundred and nineteen thousand. Eighty-six per cent less to re-read, on every turn.
  10. lesson. Write it early. Keep state in files. Load only what you need.
  11. team. Saving tokens is a side effect. The real gain is scale. This film was made by an agile cell: one person, two maestros on two accounts, a conductor, a lead agent and its helpers, on different models. The work was handed out across them, with one repository as the source of truth. When the film is done, the cell is archived, and its files stay in the library. This is how we scale.
